# LaxCraft

LaxCraft is a mobile training app that gives lacrosse players a personalised practice plan, a real glossary, and clear progress tracking — all built around their position and their game. It covers both the Men's and Women's game, so every drill, term, and quiz question is shown to the right player.

## Who built it, and why

LaxCraft was founded and built by Mariyum, a lacrosse player who wanted a training tool that actually understood the differences between the men's and women's game — rather than a generic app built around one version of the sport. It started as a prototype and has grown through several rounds of real user testing and feedback.

## What works right now (v4.6)

- **35+ drills** across the Men's and Women's game, each with technique tips and a difficulty/time/solo-or-partner breakdown
- **Illustrated glossary** of lacrosse terms, with animated diagrams showing technique (dodges, draws, scooping, and more)
- **Technique tips** on every drill, plus a two-level Top Tips section by position and game-day situation
- **A personalised survey** on first use — game, position, focus skills, and training days — that drives a tailored drill list and a day-by-day or week-by-week training plan
- **Verified / Unverified source labels** on every drill (see below)
- A Game IQ quiz, a Progress tab with goals, streaks and collectible badges, and a permanent logo and onboarding screen

## Our verification rule

Every drill in LaxCraft is labelled **Verified** or **Unverified**:

- **Verified** means the drill is sourced from an established coaching resource (youth coaching guides, recognised lacrosse training content).
- **Unverified** means it hasn't been checked against an outside source yet — usually because it came from team testing or early iteration.

We built this label into the app itself, not just our documentation, because a training app that quietly mixes solid coaching advice with unchecked ideas can waste a young player's time or teach the wrong habit. Labelling the source up front means players and coaches can always see how much to trust a drill, and it keeps us honest about what we've actually checked versus what we still need to verify.

## Roadmap
